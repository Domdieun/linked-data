For my Linked Data course project, I developed a semantic knowledge representation of an iNaturalist biodiversity dataset, focusing on bird observations, particularly Barn Swallow (Hirundo rustica) records. The original dataset contained more than 31,000 observations with information on taxonomy, locations, users, dates, media, and observation quality. I cleaned and transformed the data and mapped it to established semantic vocabularies, including Darwin Core, Dublin Core, Schema.org, and GeoNames, to make the dataset more structured, interoperable, and semantically meaningful.
A major part of the project involved ontology engineering and schema development. I designed a semantic model connecting concepts such as HumanObservation, Taxon, Person, Place, Location, MediaObject, and ObservationStatus, and defined their data properties, object properties, and hierarchical relationships. For example, observations were linked to taxonomic classifications, geographical locations, their creators, associated media, and observation-quality information. I then used SPARQL to query the resulting knowledge graph—for example, retrieving species observations and coordinates, aggregating observations by location, examining associated media, and analysing observation quality.
Through the project and the wider course, I gained practical experience with RDF/RDFS, OWL, knowledge graphs, ontology design and engineering, SPARQL, semantic repositories, GraphDB, and developing schemas for structured datasets. I also learned how Linked Data principles can be used to integrate heterogeneous data sources and potentially connect datasets such as iNaturalist with external resources such as GBIF and GeoNames.
